# Your first path

This path shows how a course works. It has four steps: some reading, a short
quiz, an exercise, and a recap. Replace the text with your own subject and keep
the shape.

## Read

Good learning starts with one small idea. Here it is: **a habit grows from a
small action that you repeat at the same time and place.** The action is easy
to do, and doing it often matters more than doing it well.

Three parts make a habit stick:

1. A **cue**, the moment that tells you to start, such as pouring your morning coffee.
2. A **routine**, the small action itself, such as writing one line in a notebook.
3. A **reward**, something that feels good right after, such as ticking a box.

Read the three parts again, then go on to the quiz. The next step opens when you
press Next.

## Check your understanding

Answer the questions. You may try again, and your best score is saved.

:::quiz{id=first-path-quiz title="Habits" pass=70}
1. What does a cue do?
   - ( ) It rewards you
   - (x) It tells you when to start
   - ( ) It makes the task harder
   > The cue is the moment that triggers the routine.
2. Which of these make a habit stick?
   - [x] A clear cue
   - [x] A small routine
   - [ ] A large goal with no plan
   - [x] A reward right after
   > A big goal on its own gives you nothing to do at a set moment.
3. Which part of a habit is the action you repeat? = routine
   > The routine is the small action itself.
:::

## Try it

Now use the idea on something real. Pick one small action you want to repeat,
and write down its cue, its routine and its reward. Then tick each item as you
finish it.

:::check
- [ ] I picked one small action
- [ ] I wrote down its cue
- [ ] I wrote down its routine
- [ ] I chose a reward
:::

## Recap

You finished the path. In short:

- A habit is a cue, a routine and a reward.
- Small and often beats big and rare.
- Write the cue down, so you notice it.
